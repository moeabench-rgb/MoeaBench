    DPF5

    The DPF5 problem has a Pareto optimal front defined by:

    \[ \boldsymbol{\textcolor{blue}{f_1^2}(X)} + \boldsymbol{\textcolor{blue}{f_2^2}(X)} + \underbrace{\boldsymbol{\textcolor{blue}{f_3^2}(X)} + \boldsymbol{....}}_{\text{M > 3} } + \boldsymbol{\textcolor{blue}{f_M^2}(X) = 1} \Longrightarrow \boldsymbol{\sum_{m=1}^M\textcolor{blue}{f_{m}^2} = 1} \]

    DPF5 is defined by the following variables:

    • M: Number of objectives in the problem
    • N: Number of decision variables
    • D: Number of essential objectives
    • K: represented by:
    \[ \boldsymbol{K}\text{ is the value of the vector } \boldsymbol{\big|X^{\large r}\big|} \text{ where all } \boldsymbol{X_i} \text{ }\boldsymbol{(X_m \in X_n)} \]

    The value of N can be obtained by the equation N = D + K - 1.

    Structure

    The problem is divided into 2 parts:

    • M-D: Where the redundant objectives are located, which may vary according to the size of M and D. It can also change depending on the decision variable x1.
    • M-D+1: Where the essential objectives of the problem are located, with a total of D objectives.

    • M-D: Redundant objectives

    Redundancy

    Multiobjective benchmark test problems usually have a Pareto front defined by M−1 dimensions, where all objectives are mutually conflicting and independent. The presence of redundant objectives makes the dimension of the Pareto optimal front degenerate, meaning smaller than M−1.

    • M = 10: problem objectives
    • D = 5: essential objectives
    • M-D = 5: redundant objectives

    X1 > 1/3

    The number of redundant objectives increases according to the value of m - d and the decision variable X1 > ⅓

    \[ \boldsymbol{\underbrace{\underbrace{\textcolor{red}{\;\underbrace{\overbrace{\gamma_1}^{f_1}}_{\textbf{redundant}}\;\;\underbrace{\overbrace{\gamma_2}^{f_2}\;\;\overbrace{\gamma_2}^{f_3}\;\;\overbrace{\gamma_2}^{f_4}\;\;\overbrace{\gamma_2}^{f_5}}_{\textbf{redundant}}}}_{\overline{m-d}}\;\;\underbrace{\;\textcolor{blue}{\underbrace{\textcolor{blue}{\overbrace{\gamma_1}^{f_6}}\;\;\textcolor{blue}{\overbrace{\gamma_2}^{f_7}}}_{\textbf{conflicting}}}\;\;\textcolor{red}{\underbrace{\overbrace{\gamma_2}^{f_8}}_{\textbf{redundant}}}\;\;\textcolor{blue}{ \underbrace{ \overbrace{\gamma_{d-1}}^{f_9}}_{\textbf{conflicting}\;}}}_{\overline{d-1}}} _{\overline{m-1}}\;\;\underbrace{\underbrace{\textcolor{blue}{\underbrace{\overbrace{\gamma_d}^{f_{10}}}_{\;\textbf{conflicting}}\;}}_{\overline{d}}}_{\overline{m}}} \]

    In the segment m - d, there are two redundant segments ( γ1 , γ2 ) totaling 5 redundant objectives. In the d division, within the d - 1 segment, the essential objectives are located, including one redundant objective γ2 when d > 4. In the m - 1 division (which includes m - d and d), the segments ( γ1 , γ2 , γd-1 ) are mutually conflicting, totaling 3 objectives.

    X1 < 1/3

    The number of redundant objectives also increases according to the value of m
